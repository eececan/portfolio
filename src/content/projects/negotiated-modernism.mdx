## The question

A manifesto is written once; a building is built somewhere. Le Corbusier's Five
Points of Architecture — pilotis, free plan, free facade, horizontal window,
roof garden — were stated as universal principles. Every architect who adopted
them did so in a particular climate, on particular ground, for particular
clients.

This project asks how much of the manifesto survives that contact, and whether
the deviations are noise or pattern. The objective was to build a computational
pipeline that quantifies the *negotiation* between the theory and the pragmatic
realities of climate, geography, and local context as interpreted by Le
Corbusier's followers.

## Case studies

Four houses: the canonical statement, and three interpretations by architects
working in hotter, sunnier places. Climate figures are 2015–2024 normals pulled
from the Open-Meteo archive for each building's coordinates.

## Operationalising the Five Points

This is the hard part of the project, and the part where a reader should push
back hardest. A principle stated in prose has to become something a computer can
measure, and each translation loses something.

**Horizontal window → glazing ratio.** Windows are hand-marked on each facade
photograph in coloured pen; the pipeline extracts the mark by HSV threshold and
computes glazed area as a fraction of facade area. Automating the area, not the
identification.

**Free plan → flood-filled room segmentation.** Floor plans are segmented into
rooms by adaptive flood fill, then scored on three criteria: does the largest
room dominate the interior (area > 15%), is partition density low (< 20%), and
is the largest room non-convex (convexity < 0.85), which distinguishes a flowing
space from a big rectangular one.

**Free facade → glass extent ratio.** Measures how completely glazing spans the
horizontal extent of the window block, on the logic that a facade freed from
load-bearing duty can run glass edge to edge.

**Roof garden → flat-roof classifier.** Roof pitch is a three-dimensional
property that a single photograph cannot recover geometrically, so the decision
is made by CLIP zero-shot classification, with a geometric "roof slope index"
running alongside as a one-sided veto for mono-pitch roofs.

supports within a horizontal band at the base of the facade, and reports the
void ratio of that band.

## What survives, and what gets renegotiated

**Free plan and free facade travel intact.** All four houses pass both. Open-plan
scores are 100 for Savoye, Canoas and Frey House II, and 70 for Kaufmann; free
facade ratios run 1.00, 0.98, 0.97 and 0.83. Whatever else changes when the
manifesto crosses an ocean, the interior spatial logic goes with it.

**The flat roof mostly survives, but not always.** Savoye, Canoas and Kaufmann
are classified flat. Frey House II is not — it uses a single-pitch roof, and the
classifier caught it through the mono-pitch veto. In the Palm Springs desert, a
sloped plane that throws shade is a better answer than a flat deck, and Frey
took it.

**The horizontal window survives but gets amplified.** This is the clearest
pattern in the data. Villa Savoye, in a 12 °C climate with 7.9 hours of daily
sun, glazes 11.8% of its facade. The three houses in warmer, sunnier places
glaze 23.5%, 27.8% and 30.4% — roughly double to triple. The ribbon window of
the manifesto becomes a glass wall once the climate rewards it.

**Pilotis is the point that does not travel.** Only Villa Savoye stands on them.
The other three sit on or into their ground — Canoas draped over a rock, the
desert houses anchored to their site.

The shape of that result is worth stating plainly: the points describing how
space is organised *inside* the building survived transplantation, while the
points describing how the building meets the ground and the sky were the ones
that got renegotiated locally. The manifesto's interior logic was portable. Its
relationship to site was not.

## Limitations

**Four buildings is not a sample.** Nothing here supports a correlation or a
statistical claim, and the glazing pattern is not even monotonic — Kaufmann sits
in a hotter climate than Canoas but glazes less. What the data supports is an
ordering observation with one clear outlier, and that is how it should be read.

**The pilotis detector is validated on one building.** The other three report
zero columns, but their analysis bands fall over terrace, pool and lawn rather
than a ground storey. Those zeros happen to match the architecture, but they are
not measurements and must not be read as any.

**Window marking is manual.** The glazing ratios rest on a person deciding, in
pen, where a window ends. The pipeline automates the area computation, not the
judgement, and no inter-annotator agreement was measured.

**Single-view photographs cannot recover roof pitch.** A pitched roof
photographed along its slope direction projects to a horizontal line and is
unrecoverable from that image alone. Frey House II was decided at a CLIP margin
of 0.03 — effectively a coin flip rescued by the geometric veto.

**The scoring thresholds are hand-set.** Area > 15%, density < 20%, convexity <
0.85 were chosen, not learned. The Villa Rotonda control shows they are not
trivially permissive, but one control is thin.

## What's next

More buildings per architect, so that individual interpretation can be separated
from climate. More controls, in both directions. Inter-annotator agreement on
the window marking. And validating the pilotis detector on buildings that are
known to stand on pilotis, so that its zeros mean something.
